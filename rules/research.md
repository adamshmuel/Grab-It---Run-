# Research and evidence

Adam requires current research and practical feasibility checks across all agreed capability areas: game and visual design, Luau and code quality, testing tools and coverage, goal-driven documentation, small task decomposition and context efficiency, plan tracking, player experience, platform behavior and security, product and monetization decisions, and maintenance and reuse.

- Do not guess or treat remembered information as current evidence. Use prior knowledge to frame questions, then verify decision-relevant claims with current sources and project evidence before relying on them.
- Search the web proactively to fill gaps and refine the approach. Match sources to the question: official documentation, specifications, and release notes for platform capabilities; live game data, actual gameplay, creator disclosures, independent analyses, and player feedback for market and experience research. Do not limit research to create.roblox.com or any single source. Check dates, applicable versions, and limitations; recent publication alone does not establish relevance or correctness.
- For competitor research, define the relevant category and comparable mechanics before selecting games. Search with focused queries and cross-check independent evidence. Include relevant successful games and close mechanical comparators; do not assume a broad list of popular Roblox games represents our category or proves which design choices caused success.
- Record the metric, source, date, and observation period behind a ranking. Distinguish concurrent players, visits, retention, revenue rankings, and reported revenue. Do not infer earnings from popularity, visits, product prices, or paid-item availability. Label third-party revenue estimates as estimates with their methodology and limitations; if earnings are unavailable, say so.
- Start visual research with a small, purposeful selection of actual gameplay screenshots. Reuse adequate saved references instead of repeatedly retrieving or analyzing the same material. Use short, targeted video segments only when still images cannot answer the question, such as animation, pacing, transitions, or timed feedback; avoid full-video analysis by default. Do not claim measured resource savings without measurements.
- Assess graphics and presentation across relevant gameplay moments, with source links and video timestamps where applicable. Examine the environment, UI, camera, lighting, animation, effects, visual clarity, and player feedback using evidence that can actually demonstrate each property. Icons, thumbnails, trailers, and search-result images are discovery aids, not sufficient evidence of the playing experience. Verify that references show the intended game and a relevant version; distinguish screenshots, watched footage, and direct play.
- When reference images are downloaded, retain them locally under `references/competitors/<game-slug>/<topic>/`, for example, `environment`, `ui`, or `feedback`. Use descriptive filenames. Keep a per-game `index.md` recording each image's filename, source page and original media URL when available, creator or publisher when known, publication/capture date when known, retrieval date, scene/topic, and why it matters. Mark missing metadata as unknown. Link reused images rather than saving duplicates. These are research references, not game assets.
- Root `references/` remains ignored by Git. Keep conclusions and source links needed to reproduce the research in the relevant tracked research document; do not depend solely on ignored images or add them forcibly to Git. Create reference folders only when collecting actual material, not as empty placeholders.
- Turn competitor observations into concrete, scoped implications for our goals, such as readability or feedback quality. Record what was observed separately from interpretation, and evaluate how to achieve an appropriate standard within our resources. Do not copy assets or treat a competitor's scope as an automatic requirement.
- Read the relevant project skills. A skill is guidance to verify, not proof that its recommendations are current or that a capability has been demonstrated here.
- Check feasibility against the actual project environment. Where a decision depends on behavior, use a focused, authorized experiment or test. A working example elsewhere is not proof that our integration works.
- Distinguish sourced facts, local observations, proposals, and unresolved assumptions. Record material sources, the date checked, relevant versions, what was actually exercised, and remaining gaps in the relevant task document. If verification is unavailable, state the uncertainty and do not present the claim as established.
- Use human playtests and observed player behavior to assess clarity and enjoyment. Documentation and automated tests alone cannot establish that the game is fun or will retain players or earn revenue.
- Research applies to process choices too: documentation, task size, context usage, and progress tracking must be assessed against observed results. Do not invent token savings, coverage levels, or completion evidence.
- Keep research focused on the next authorized decision. Reuse recorded findings when still applicable; recheck when versions, requirements, conflicting evidence, or uncertainty make them unreliable. Avoid broad repeated searches that do not affect a decision.
- Research does not authorize implementation, installations, Studio operations, spending, publishing, or delegation. If practical verification requires an unauthorized action, record that gap and obtain the necessary permission before acting.

## Competitive quality gates — Adam's decision, 2026-10-23

- **During planning, before approving the visual specification or implementation plan:** inspect relevant actual gameplay screenshots from successful comparable games. Define the required visual standard using specific references and concrete choices for color variety, shape variety, object detail, environment, UI, camera, and feedback. Do not treat readability, simple geometry, or successful tests alone as competitive quality. Check that the proposed approach is feasible within the project's tools, scope, and costs before relying on it. Record the evidence and limitations in small linked documents.
- **After implementation, before human player tests and the remaining acceptance tests:** compare actual gameplay from our game against those references again, on desktop and simulated mobile. Record pass/fail, evidence, material gaps, and Adam's assessment. Existing technical test results remain valid only within their recorded scope; they do not waive this gate.
- If the visual standard is not met, stop the downstream acceptance sequence. Revise the relevant specification and obtain Adam's approval before implementing changed design choices. Focused TDD, code review, and checks necessary to build or assess the visual change remain required; they are not a reason to run unrelated acceptance or player tests early.
- Current V1 failed this visual gate: Adam identified insufficient variety of colors and shapes and overly simple objects on 2026-10-23. Do not describe this only as unverified, or continue downstream tests until the revised look passes.

- For this project's visual redesign, Adam requires usable Roblox-native models and actual Studio evidence. Do not spend tokens producing SVG illustrations or image-generation mockups as substitutes. Prefer assets that remain reusable in the project; label any untested proposal accurately.
